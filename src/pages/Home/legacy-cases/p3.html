PROJECT 03

Digital Asset Trading & UX Strategy

Jan 2021 – May 2021 · Product Intern (QA) · Cross-functional team of 8
Pre-A · 300M JPY raised Internship

REC & Carbon Credit trading platform. Designing intuitiveness that persuades institutional investors — escaping conventional HTS UI.

01. Context & Problem

Conventional HTS UI missed the essence of energy trading. Joined the MVP as QA Intern on a small team building a blockchain platform for REC and Carbon Credits.

Issue Discovery

  • Trigger Human error from buy/sell button colors set opposite to local convention (US vs Asia). The misclick wasn't user fault — the interface contradicted muscle memory.
  • Root Cause Customers were not day traders but institutional managers handling portfolios. They needed accuracy and condition comparison, not speed. Standard HTS density imposed unnecessary cognitive load.

Insight: design an experience where the user trades intuitively, without hesitation — structure first, speed second.

02. Hypothesis & Solution

Hypothesis: a bulletin-style structure with explicit information hierarchy reduces error risk and increases trust. Goal: zero malfunction, minimised learning cost.

Action · Logic & Prioritisation

  • Data-Driven Persuasion Convinced the planning team with color-culture differences and F-pattern eye-tracking data. Demonstrated that list visibility, not chart density, drives accuracy for this user.
  • Engineering Collaboration Compromised on CSS / layout adjustments instead of full redevelopment. Held structural intuitiveness and data integrity as the immovable priorities to meet the MVP deadline.

05. Retrospective — KPT
Keep

Data-driven persuasion. Used eye-tracking data and cultural conventions as objective evidence to move stakeholders past "this is how we always did it."

Problem

Cost of late discovery. A fatal design error surfaced only at QA phase, forcing a hotfix path instead of a clean redesign.

Try

Shift left. Quality control belongs at planning, not QA. Run usability tests at the wireframe stage to minimise rework cost.
