PROJECT 02

Hyper-local P2P Delivery

Aug 2024 – Nov 2024 · PM & Python Dev · Team of 2

Campus gig economy that converts movement data into a logistics network.

01. Problem Discovery

Potential demand explodes — last-mile trapped in inefficiency.

Where the cost lives

  • Inefficiency Riders move to catch orders. On campus — hills, restricted entry, long vertical time — last-mile cost is abnormally high.
  • Unmet Demand Students want coffee but break time (10–15 min) is too short for a round trip. Demand stays latent because the fixed cost can't be amortised.

Insight: no need for professional riders. Students moving to that building anyway can deliver — supply is already on the route.

02. Solution Strategy

Route-based dispatch, not location-based.

Paradigm Shift

  • As-Is Rider moves to order origin → pickup → delivery. Move to deliver.
  • To-Be Orderer picks last-mile destination, system matches a student already moving there. Deliver while moving.

Mechanism: student couriers pick up coffee on their way to class. Delivery cost collapses; mover efficiency maxes out.

03. Market Validation

Beyond technical feasibility — verifying willingness to participate.

user survey · n=100 68% positive response: "will deliver if route matches 90%" · framed as monetising idle time
·
unit economics < 1,000 KRW matched fee per delivery · 70% cheaper than the 3,000 KRW standard. Validates a micro-delivery market.
04. Algorithm Comparison

Same start and pickup. Legacy solves scattered drops as a shortest tour. Campus fixes the student's endpoint first, then places drops on that route.

Legacy
After the same pickup, scattered drops define the path. The route becomes a delivery tour.
Campus (Proposed)
The endpoint stays fixed. Drops are placed between student and class, so walking carries the delivery.
05. Tech & Verification

VRP simulation using Google OR-Tools.

Approach

  • Algorithm Derived optimal routes with the Google OR-Tools library, matching multiple students (vehicles) against orders (nodes).
  • Constraints Modeled real campus variables — time window (class schedule), vertical time (building entry), walking speed — not just horizontal distance.
simulation −40% total distance reduced. Python prototype on real campus map data using OR-Tools route matching.
06. Outcome & Impact

The 40% distance cut converts directly into delivery cost.

cost per delivery 3,000 → 900 KRW 70% saving proven against the standard 3,000 KRW rate.
07. Retrospective — KPT
Keep

Route-based, not location-based. Designed a model that uses idle resources without adding labour input. Supply is already moving.

Problem

Supply-demand sync. Orders and couriers surge during breaks and vanish during classes — couriers also have to attend class. The peak-time supply crunch was structural, not a tuning problem.

Try

Dynamic pricing — 1.5× incentive during in-class hours to pull supply across the trough. Pickup zones near classrooms to drive wait time toward zero.
