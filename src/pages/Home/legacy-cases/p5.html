01. Redefining the Automobile's Value Layers

"What is a car in 15 years? Not a product — a layer stack."

We decomposed the car into five value layers and analyzed which would be disrupted by EV + full autonomous driving.

  • Powertrain LayerICE → EV transition accelerates. Battery supply chain becomes the bottleneck, not engine technology.
  • Software LayerFull autonomous driving eliminates the driver. The car becomes a software-defined space, not a driving machine.
  • Ownership LayerCost structure reversal: taxi at ¥1,050/10km (vs ¥3,500 today) makes sharing economically dominant over ownership.
  • Ride Experience LayerWith no driver seat, the interior becomes a "pure space" — reconfigurable for transit, commerce, work, or healthcare.
  • Brand InvisibilityNobody knows who manufactured their subway car. With ride-hailing, people say "call an Uber" — not "call a car manufacturer." The platform owns the brand; the manufacturer becomes invisible.

Core Thesis

"When EV + autonomous driving resolve cost and availability constraints, the economic rationality structurally reverses from ownership to sharing. The manufacturer that doesn't own the platform layer becomes a commodity supplier — 'Car A' on someone else's dispatch screen."

Without the platform layer, the manufacturer loses data, customer identity, and pricing power — becoming an interchangeable commodity.

The "Car A" Problem: Today, consumers already choose ride-hailing services (apps), not vehicle manufacturers. They don't know or care whether the arriving car is made by any particular brand. This is not a future risk — it is a present-day behavioral pattern that will only deepen with full autonomy. The brand loyalty that protected automakers for a century is structurally dissolving.
03. From Manufacturer to "Movable Space Developer"

"The car is not a vehicle. It's a reconfigurable space on wheels."

Peak-hour supply obligation creates massive idle assets during off-peak. The solution: redefine the vehicle as a variable-purpose space.

peak

Peak Hours → Transit

During commute hours, the full fleet operates as on-demand transit infrastructure. Autonomous dispatch ensures universal accessibility with guaranteed wait-time SLAs.

retail

Off-Peak → Mobile Commerce

When transit demand drops, vehicles transform into mobile retail, food service, medical clinics, or private offices. Tenants rent space by the hour — no fixed storefront needed.

revenue

Dual Revenue Stream

Passengers pay for transit. Tenants pay for space. Matching fees and rental income create a stable revenue floor independent of transit demand volatility.

Business Model Shift

"The role transforms from vehicle manufacturer to movable real estate developer — planning, operating, and monetizing mobile spaces across an entire city. Asset utilization maximized. Urban resource allocation optimized."

04. Platform Gap Analysis

The client's in-house MaaS app was a near-total market failure.

dominant map platform 84.1% usage rate · nationwide · tens of millions MAU
vs
client's MaaS app ~0.1% 14 regions only · ~110K users · unmeasurable share

Root Causes of Failure

  • Review Data VoidNo restaurant/venue ratings — the core driver of destination search behavior. Users had no reason to open the app.
  • Cold Start TrapWithout users, no data accumulates. Without data, platform value doesn't grow. A classic chicken-and-egg failure.
  • UX FragmentationBuilt on top of a third-party map API, resulting in a disjointed user experience that couldn't compete with native map apps.

Opportunity: the domestic market is uniquely fragmented — ride-hailing, reviews, and transit exist as separate apps. An aggregator unifying these verticals could leapfrog the map incumbent.

Not "buy three companies" — a single pipeline where each acquisition solves a prerequisite for the next.

ACQUISITION 1resource

Lithium Mining Company

Resource Security · ~¥500B

A top-tier hard-rock lithium producer with world-class reserves, green extraction (zero-carbon, dry processing, hydroelectric power), and immediate production capacity.
ProblemEV scale-up requires lithium volumes the client cannot secure at market prices. Mine development takes 10+ years from scratch.
SolutionFull acquisition + on-site refinery construction. Shift from market-linked procurement to mining-cost basis — a structural cost advantage.
EffectSupply autonomy (break China-dependent refining chain), cost leadership in battery economics, and a strategic resource hedge for the entire EV fleet.
ACQUISITION 2ride

Dominant Ride-Hailing App

User Base + Dispatch Infra · ~¥150B–250B

The market leader in domestic ride-hailing: 70%+ metro utilization rate, 25M+ downloads, 10M+ monthly rides. Brand recognition equivalent to "ride-hailing = this app."
ProblemThe client has no direct customer touchpoint in mobility services. Dispatch, pricing, and user data are all controlled by third-party platforms.
SolutionAcquire the irreproducible asset: market share + brand trust. Backend integration only — preserve the existing brand, UX, and user base intact.
EvolutionEvolve from a dispatch tool into a destination-driven life portal — "Where do you want to go?" before "How do you get there?"
ACQUISITION 3food

National Restaurant Review Platform

Content + Decision Data · ~¥300B–430B

~97M MAU. Decades of accumulated review data and venue ratings that represent the single most trusted source for dining decisions in the domestic market. This trust cannot be replicated with capital or time.
Why Food?"Food" is the #1 driver of location-based search behavior. It generates the highest-frequency, most habitual user engagement — the ideal anchor for a life portal.
IntegrationMerge "where to go" (reviews) + "how to get there" (dispatch) into a single flow. Destination decision and movement become seamless.
Data AssetUser preference data + movement data + consumption data — combined, these create the behavioral intelligence to power personalized mobility and tenant matching.
06. Convergence: The Super App as Platform

"Don't ask users to download a new app. Update their existing one."

Instead of building from scratch, unify the three acquired apps via coordinated updates. Day 1: the platform launches with tens of millions of active users. Cold start problem eliminated.

Why This Works

  • No Cold StartExisting users of all three apps migrate via update — no new download required. The platform launches at full scale.
  • "Search → Decide → Move" LoopUsers search for a destination (reviews), make a decision, and are immediately dispatched — all within one interface. Every search generates movement. Every movement generates data.
  • Behavioral Data FlywheelPreference data (what you like) + movement data (where you go) + consumption data (what you buy) combine into the behavioral intelligence layer that powers personalized dispatch, tenant matching, and urban space optimization.
  • Structural Moat vs. Map IncumbentsThe dominant map platform lacks proprietary review content and ride-hailing infrastructure. In the uniquely fragmented domestic market, the aggregator model has a structural advantage the map incumbent cannot replicate.
07. Global Expansion Strategy

Existing Markets: Governance Tightening

In markets with established ride-hailing partners, incrementally increase equity stakes to gain governance control. Ensure strategic alignment and ecosystem integration without hostile disruption.

New Markets: Infrastructure-First Entry

In greenfield smart city developments, embed the super app as the standard mobility OS from the urban planning stage. Establish as local infrastructure before competitors can enter.

08. Retrospective (KPT)

"What I took away from building a ¥1T argument in one week."

Keep

Structure-First Framing: Started from industry structure (ownership → sharing → platform dependency), not from "which company should we buy." The framing made M&A targets feel inevitable, not arbitrary.

Keep

Pipeline Thinking: Three acquisitions forming one pipeline — resources enable fleet, user base enables platform, content enables engagement. A connected system, not a shopping list.

Problem

Execution Depth on Variable Space: The "movable real estate" concept lacked quantitative backing — tenant economics, fleet utilization, and regulatory feasibility were conceptual, not simulated.

Try

Simulation-Backed Strategy: Pair structural arguments with quantitative models — demand forecasting, fleet utilization simulation, financial scenario planning.
